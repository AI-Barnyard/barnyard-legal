New Grad Job Search Tracker & Salary Negotiation Kit

Everything a new graduate needs to run a real job search: offer comparison with cost-of-living adjustment, application tracker, and negotiation scripts.

Not legal or tax advice. These templates and scripts are educational examples based on common practice. Use them as a starting point, not gospel. Negotiate respectfully and in good faith. If the company has equity, understand vesting schedules and consult a tax professional on how it's valued.

03Salary Negotiation Scripts

These are word-for-word responses to the most common pushback lines. Adapt to your situation, but keep the structure: acknowledge, explain value, request.

When they say: "We have a strict band for entry-level."
"I appreciate the transparency. I'm excited about this role. Based on the scope — [mention 2–3 specific responsibilities] — and my [specific skill or project], I believe $[X] is fair. Can you see any room to move?"
Why it works: You acknowledged the constraint but showed you researched the role, not just the title. You aren't asking for the moon.
When they say: "We can't do more base, but we can add $X bonus."
"Thank you. I want to make sure I understand the bonus structure. What's the historical payout rate, and how is it measured? Also, would you have flexibility to increase the signing bonus instead, since that's guaranteed?"
Why it works: Bonuses can evaporate. You're asking smart questions and showing you know the difference. Signing bonus is money in hand, and it's usually easier to negotiate than base or bonus payout.
When they say: "Everyone starts at the same salary."
"I understand consistency is important. I also see this as an investment in me. My background in [skill/project] means I'll contribute faster than a typical grad. What does 'same' look like, and is there any flexibility for proven expertise?"
Why it works: You're reframing the negotiation from "I want more money" to "I'll deliver more value." You also forced them to name the number.
When they say: "You don't have experience to ask for that."
"I hear that. My ask is based on market research for this role in this city, not seniority. I'm invested in proving myself — I know I'm early in my career. What would make you comfortable with this number?"
Why it works: You separated market value from experience. You also asked them to own the decision instead of shutting down.
When they say: "We can't compete with the Big Tech companies."
"I'm not trying to be a Big Tech company. I chose you because [mission/culture/learning/specific reason]. My ask reflects your city and market, not SF salaries. Here's what market research shows for [role] in [city]: [range]. Can we find a number in that range?"
Why it works: You complimented the company, set a boundary (you aren't chasing startup pipe dreams), and gave them concrete data. Bring a printed salary guide (levels.fyi, glassdoor, payscale) to back it up if needed.
Last resort: "I need to think about it. When can we talk tomorrow?"
Don't decide on the spot. Sleep on it. Call back the next day and either accept or make a counteroffer. Most companies expect one round of back-and-forth.
Why it works: Silence and delay are your friends. Let them sit with your counteroffer overnight. They often come back with a bump.
