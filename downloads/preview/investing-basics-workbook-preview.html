01What You're Actually Buying

Three terms cover most of what a beginner needs to understand.

A stock
A share of ownership in one company. If that one company does badly, so do you. This is why holding only a handful of individual stocks is riskier than it looks.
An index fund
A single fund that holds hundreds or thousands of companies at once, tracking a whole market (like the S&P 500). One purchase spreads your money across everything in that index — that spread is the entire point.
A bond
A loan to a government or company that pays interest. Historically less volatile than stocks and lower returning. Its job in a portfolio is to cushion the drops, not to make you rich.
