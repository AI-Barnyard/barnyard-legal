Why Study Blocks Matter

Your brain learns through focused repetition, not cramming.

A 90-minute study block beats three 30-minute sessions because you reach deep focus. Assign 2-3 blocks per week per course. Treat them like class meetings—non-negotiable, same time every week.

Standard guideline: 3 hours outside class per 1 hour in class. 15 hours of class per week? Budget 45 hours outside. That's achievable if you focus.

Tip: Track your total load. If over 60 hours/week, you're overcommitted. Reduce course load or adjust expectations.

Spaced Repetition: The Science of Memory

Review material on this schedule to move knowledge from short-term to long-term memory.

Spacing: Review after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 5 weeks. Each review reinforces the material more deeply than the previous one.

Example: Learn topic Monday → review Tuesday (1 day) → Thursday (3 days) → next Monday (1 week) → 2 weeks later → 5 weeks later.

This schedule beats cramming by embedding knowledge permanently in your memory, not just temporarily for an exam.

Three Non-Negotiable Habits

Sleep, exercise, and screen limits—these drive learning success.

Sleep (7-8 hours)
Most learning consolidates during sleep. Missing sleep to study is a net loss. Worse recall, slower processing, poor decisions. Sleep is non-negotiable.
Exercise (20+ minutes)
Improves focus, mood, and memory retention. A 20-minute walk before studying noticeably improves focus.
Screen time limit
Study + 1 hour leisure max. Prevents burnout and protects your mental health. Strict boundaries actually increase your productivity.
